Auditing the scoring tables
6,400 elite performances, 16 events, Kruskal-Wallis

Testing the World Athletics points tables against the elite

The best 5000 m runners outscore the best javelin throwers by 68.5 points

World Athletics points are meant to make a 1200-point shot put equal to a 1200-point 100 m. Combined events, cross-event awards and every comparison of athletes from different events rely on that. I scraped 6,400 performances from the 2023 and 2024 top lists, each with the points it was awarded, and compared the median score of each event's top 30. If the tables were level, those medians would sit close together. They range from 1203.5 in the javelin to 1272 in the 5000 m, and a Kruskal-Wallis test rejects equal distributions (H = 236, p ≈ 10−42).

The question

A claim the sport's own data can test

The scoring tables sit underneath every cross-event comparison: a decathlete's javelin against their 1500 m, a shot putter against a miler for an award, or a country's strength across events, as in my national strength profiles. Any bias in the tables carries into all of them.

If the tables are level, the best performers in each event should earn about the same number of points, because each group is at the front of its own event. The World Athletics top lists publish the points each mark was awarded, so this can be checked without recomputing anything.

Limitations

What this doesn't prove

An event's elite scores depend on two things at once: how the table converts marks to points, and how deep the event's current field is. A thinly contested event shows a lower ceiling even under a perfectly fair table, because fewer athletes are pushing its front. Javelin and hammer may well be thinner fields, and this study can't separate the two causes.

So the supported reading is that equal points don't currently mean equal elite standing across events. It does not show the tables are miscoded. Separating calibration from depth would need each table checked against a fixed frontier, such as the world record, and that is not done here.

The top 30 per event pools men and women, so one sex can fill most of an event's elite slice. Two seasons is a short window, and the H and p values come from the raw scrape, which is git-ignored and not in the repository.

Where it matters

What it changes in practice

A points-based shortlist for a cross-event award favours distance runners over throwers who stand equally high in their own events. The decathlon and heptathlon use the same kind of conversion, so a similar tilt there is worth testing, though this study only covers the individual-event tables. And any national-strength measure built on WA points, including mine, inherits whatever tilt the tables have.
