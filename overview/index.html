Auditing the scoring tables
6,400 elite performances, 16 events, Kruskal-Wallis

Testing the World Athletics points tables against the elite

The best 5000 m runners outscore the best javelin throwers by 68.5 points

World Athletics points are meant to make a 1200-point shot put worth the same as a 1200-point 100 m. If that held, the top 30 marks in every event would earn about the same points. In the 2023 and 2024 top lists they don't: the median ranges from 1203.5 in the javelin to 1272 in the 5000 m, and running events sit high while jumps and throws sit low.

The question

A claim the sport's own data can test

The scoring tables sit under every cross-event comparison: a decathlete's javelin against their 1500 m, a shot putter against a miler for an award, or a country's strength across events, as in my national strength profiles. Any bias in the tables carries into all of them.

The World Athletics top lists publish the points each mark was awarded, so the check needs no recomputing. I scraped the top 100 performances per event for men and women in 2023 and 2024, 6,400 marks across 16 events, and took the 30 highest scores in each event as its elite slice. A Kruskal-Wallis test rejects the idea that the 16 slices come from one distribution (H = 236, p ≈ 10−42).

Limitations

What this doesn't prove

An event's elite scores depend on two things at once: how the table converts marks to points, and how deep the event's current field is. A thinly contested event shows a lower ceiling even under a perfectly fair table, because fewer athletes are pushing its front. Javelin and hammer may well be thinner fields, and this study can't separate the two causes.

So the supported reading is that equal points don't currently mean equal elite standing across events. Whether any one table is miscoded is a separate question. Answering it would need each table checked against a fixed frontier, such as the world record, and that is not done here.

The top 30 per event pools men and women, so one sex can fill most of an event's elite slice. Two seasons is a short window, and a different pair of years could reorder events near the norm. H and p come from the raw scrape, which is git-ignored, so only the per-event table is reproducible from the committed files.

Where it matters

What it changes in practice

A points-based shortlist for a cross-event award favors distance runners over throwers who stand just as high in their own events. The decathlon and heptathlon use the same kind of conversion, so a similar tilt there is worth testing, though this study only covers the individual-event tables. Any national-strength measure built on WA points, including mine, inherits whatever tilt the tables have.

Where the numbers come from

Data

Sample
6,400 performances across 16 events
Points
The WA score each mark was awarded ("Results Score"), not recomputed

Top 30 scores per event; event median compared with the median of medians

Kruskal-Wallis H test (SciPy) on the 16 top-30 distributions
